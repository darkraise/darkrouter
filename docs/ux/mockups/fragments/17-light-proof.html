Light mode is first-class, so it gets a proof rather than a gesture. This board renders light whatever the T toggle says, so the two modes can be read against each other inside one document. Every number printed below was measured — the luminance figures from the rendered pixels of a Chrome screenshot, the contrast ratios computed from the token hexes the shipped stylesheet actually declares — and where a measurement contradicts what this screen was briefed to say, the measurement is what is printed. Sampled from two screenshots of the overview screen at the same coordinate pair — x 700, y 70 on a panel and y 330 on a well — one rendered dark and one light, decoded pixel by pixel. The ordering reverses: the recessed surface is the darkest thing in the dark frame and the brightest thing in the light one. That is the structural rule, and it is the reason a screen cannot be relit by lightening every token in place.

The measurement also reports something the rule alone does not. The light step is far weaker — 1.045:1 against dark's 1.157:1, a separation of five values out of 255 rather than eighteen. Light mode keeps the polarity but spends most of its separation budget on the 1px --hairline around the well rather than on the value step inside it. Every recessed region in light is carried by its border.

The same eight rows the ladder specimen carries, in light. Ranks 04–08 are the termination rule: a 5px hollow mark in --hairline-subtle sitting on the well, which is the faintest deliberate mark in the product and the one this screen exists to check.

It survives, and by a narrower margin than the dark rendering rather than a wider one: --hairline-subtle against --well measures 1.39:1 in dark and 1.32:1 in light. The loss is 5%, not the collapse the brief for this screen anticipated — light's well is pure white and its hairline is a pale grey, but dark's well is nearly black and its hairline nearly as dark, so neither mode was ever relying on much. The skipped mark's --bezel stroke gives up more: 4.11:1 dark against 3.42:1 light, and it clears the 3:1 non-text floor in both. The terminated mark clears nothing, and is not asked to: it reports the absence of a decision rather than a decision, the spine beneath it says the same thing at the same weight, and the row's text carries the rest. A mark that has to be found is held to the floor; a mark whose whole job is to stop asking for attention is not.

Measured against --panel in light, which is the surface a UI mark lands on, except --focus-ring, measured against --ground because a focus ring is drawn outside the control it belongs to. --primary, --success and --warning need 3:1 as non-text marks; --destructive needs 4.5:1 because it is used as text. All five shipped values fail the floor they are held to, and the middle column is what that failure measures.

The two dashed slots are deliberate. Spec §7 states a hex for sky-500, emerald-500 and amber-500 and names only a palette step for the other two, so those get their measured ratio and no swatch: rendering one would mean guessing a value the source does not give, and an invented specimen on a proof screen is worse than a gap.

One number here contradicts the brief this screen was written from, which expected --success to be repaired to 3.70:1. It measures 5.25:1. The green was strengthened after that brief was written, when a set review ruled it had to clear AA as body text and not merely the 3:1 non-text floor. The screen prints what the stylesheet does.

4 — Input focus in light

44 · Input focus — styles.css:10749: inputs use --primary, not --focus-ring. Fixing --focus-ring alone does not fix form controls.

2.86:1 against the field's own well · 2.74:1 against the panel behind it. Both under the 3:1 floor.

repaired — ring from --focus

sk-dr-v1-9f2c

4.11:1 against the field's own well · 3.93:1 against the panel behind it. Both clear it.

A focus ring is drawn across two surfaces at once — the control it outlines and the panel around it — so it is measured against both, and the weaker of the two is the one that counts. The shipped ring fails on both.

This is the control the library defect actually breaks, and the reason --focus-ring is not the token to fix. Form controls take their indicator from --primary; repairing --focus-ring alone leaves every text field, select and textarea in every consuming application with a sub-3:1 ring in light mode while the audit reports the ring token as fixed.

5 — The cooling mark's headroom

55 · Cooling headroom — 3.02:1, clears the floor by 0.02. Accepted deliberately.

--state-cooling against --panel in light: 3.02:1. The gauge spans 0.20 of ratio; the margin is the gap between the two rules.

Amber is the hardest hue to bring over the non-text floor against a near-white surface without turning it brown, and 3.02:1 is what this one manages. The margin is 0.02 — small enough that a future panel token a shade lighter would put it under, which is why the pip and the skip mark are the only two places amber is allowed, and why neither of them carries meaning that colour alone conveys. The cooling mark keeps a hollow ring and a centre dot precisely so that the colour is the second channel and not the only one.

Against --well the same mark measures 3.16:1, so a cooling mark inside a ladder has slightly more room than one on a panel. The smaller of the two is the number the decision was made on.
