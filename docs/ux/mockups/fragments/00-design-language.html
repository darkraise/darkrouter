22 · Three-way colour split — coral is BRAND, --trace is the router's VERDICT, state colours are provider IDENTITY. Three carve-outs are named below; a state colour used for anything not on that list is a bug.

Left: three provider identity pips — what each provider IS. Right: the router's verdict on the same request — what it DECIDED, in --trace and --legend. A healthy provider (groq) can still be skipped; a degraded one (cerebras) can still serve. State colour never appears in the gutter; --trace never appears on a pip.

A pip carries one of the four states GET /api/overview emits, and the wording is the API's rather than the language's: healthy, DEGRADED, disabled, unconfigured. Degraded and cooling are not synonyms and the row above shows why — cerebras reads degraded because one of its credentials is cooling until 14:22. The credential is the thing that cools; the provider is the thing that degrades. That is also why the ladder mark for a passed-over candidate stays --state-cooling: down there the subject really is one credential.

Disabled and unconfigured take no state colour at all, because neither is a health reading — nobody has measured anything. They split the way the ladder marks split: disabled is filled --legend, a thing that exists and is switched off; unconfigured is a hollow --legend ring, a thing with nothing in it yet.

And a fourth colour that is none of these three. CORAL is BRAND: it marks where you are in the nav and which control is the primary action on a screen, and it means nothing about the system's state. It is kept out of the ladder gutter and off every pip deliberately — coral sits between amber and red in hue, so a coral routing mark beside an amber cooling mark and a red failure would be three warm hues doing three unrelated jobs in one column.

Three carve-outs are named, and only three. Each is a use of a state colour that is not a provider's health, and each is listed here because a rule with unlisted exceptions is not a rule.

DESTRUCTIVE AFFORDANCE — --state-failed on a control whose action removes something: a delete button's border and hover text. Defensible precisely because a destructive click produces a failed-shaped result — the row is gone — but it is a named thing, not a variant reading of health, and it never appears on a pip or a ladder mark.

REQUEST OUTCOME — --state-healthy and --state-failed on a finished request's or probe's status, in the log and on a badge. A provider's health is a rolling judgement about a thing that persists; an outcome is a verdict on one event that is over. They are different facts wearing the same two colours, which is legible only because an outcome never appears on a pip and a health reading never appears in a status column.

ATTENTION — --state-cooling on a condition that is neither a failure nor a provider state: a stale discovery sweep, a warning chip, an alias that resolves to nothing, a value the operator has not set yet. Amber is the language's "look at this, nothing has broken" colour, and cooling is the provider-shaped member of that family rather than its whole meaning. It is the widest of the three carve-outs and the one worth policing: amber on anything that has actually failed is a bug, and so is amber used decoratively.

What none of the three permit: a state colour in the ladder gutter reporting a routing decision, or --trace on a provider pip. That boundary has no exceptions.

55 · Surface — cards sit ON the ground and stack upward. Border + radius + --shadow-soft; separation is a gutter, not a seam.

panel

⌘K

an overlay — --shadow-elevated, the menu and dialog step

0px

8px — --radius

12px — --radius-lg

An overlay is a card lifted further, not a different kind of surface: it takes --surface-2 and --shadow-elevated, and in light mode where there is no room to go brighter than a white card, the shadow and the border do the whole job. An inset region behaves the same wherever it appears — a dropdown's search box and a dialog's data region read the same value as one inside a card on the page.

RULING — radius is a scale, not a constant: 4px on a small box, 6px on a chip, 8px on a card, 12px on the chrome. It matches darkraise-ui's --radius at data-radius="rounded" and Tailwind's derived md/sm exactly, so the implementation sets one axis and writes no radius rule. Round is reserved for a STATUS DOT and for nothing else: the state pip, the cooling mark's centre dot and the stale-discovery ring. Everything that carries a silhouette reading stays square-cornered — the four ladder marks depend on filled-versus-hollow and square-versus-round to say what happened, and a circular ladder mark would collide with the pip vocabulary. A circle used as a corner treatment is still a bug, and so is --radius on a mark small enough that 8px makes it one.

Surfaces STACK UPWARD. A card is lighter than the ground it sits on, in both modes. The INSET inside a card is where that stops in light: nothing sits above white, so it steps down to --surface-2 instead and is read from its border rather than its fill. The previous language cut wells DOWN into the panel and inverted that ordering between modes; this one does not, which is why light here is a palette swap and not a polarity flip, and why the light proof measures contrast rather than direction.

Elevation is real and is spent in four places: --shadow-soft on a card, --shadow-elevated under a menu or dialog, --shadow-elev for the one lifted panel that also carries an inset highlight, and --shadow-warm, the coral-tinted one, reserved for the brand mark. A fifth, --shadow-focus, is the 3px coral ring a focused control takes. Radius runs 4 / 6 / 8 / 12px by element size
