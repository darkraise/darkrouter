Light mode is first-class, so it gets a proof rather than a gesture. This board renders light whatever the T toggle says, so the two modes can be read against each other inside one document. Under this language surfaces stack upward in both modes, so light is a palette swap by design and there is no polarity to verify — what needs verifying instead is that the palette holds its floors, because the one it was adopted from does not. Every ratio is computed from the hexes each stylesheet declares, against --surface in that mode — white in light, the card grey in dark. Seven of the nine sat under the floor their role is held to: a mark needs 3:1 and text needs 4.5:1. Two already cleared it and moved anyway, because the tier below them had to move and two greys a quarter of a step apart are one grey.

The worst of them is worth naming. 9router's light success green measures 2.54:1 and its amber 2.15:1 — both under the 3:1 floor that applies to a shape, not just to text. A provider list that says "connected" in that green on a white card is stating the most load-bearing fact on the screen in a colour a third of readers cannot separate from the background.

The card lifts off the ground in both modes and carries a border, a radius and --shadow-soft. The inset region behaves differently in the two: in dark it continues upward, in light it has nowhere left to go above white and steps down to --surface-2 instead. That is the honest consequence of a white card, and it is why an inset in light is read from its border rather than its fill.

3.19:1. The gauge spans one full point of ratio; the margin is the gap between the two rules. 9router ships this at 2.15:1, which would sit left of the floor line entirely.

Amber is the hardest hue to bring over the floor against a near-white surface without turning brown, and 3.19:1 is what this one manages — better than the 3.02:1 the previous language accepted, and still the tightest value in the set. The mark keeps a hollow ring and a centre dot so colour is its second channel and never its only one.
