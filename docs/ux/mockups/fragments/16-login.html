401 Sign-in failed. Check the username and password and try again.

The same sentence answers a wrong username, a wrong password and a console nobody has claimed yet. Which of the three it was is deliberately absent: telling them apart would leak whether an account even exists to someone who has not proven they hold one.

A token bucket per address — five attempts a minute, burst of ten — plus a global cap of four bcrypt verifications in flight at once, so a flood of guesses costs CPU rather than nothing. Past the bucket, the answer is 429 "too many login attempts; try again later" with a Retry-After — a third wording, but one about the rate limit, never about which credential was wrong.

3 unclaimed 3 · Unclaimed — the state that replaces last version's closed sign-in. GET /api/auth/status reports configured:false while the database holds no account, and the SPA renders this claim form in place of a login. POST /api/auth/setup {"username","password","confirm"} takes the same same-origin check and spends from the same per-address rate bucket as sign-in, but never claims the global four-slot bcrypt cap login does; it requires a password of at least 12 characters and a username of at most 64, and answers 409 "the console has already been set up" if another claim already won — the insert that decides that is atomic, so two claims racing cannot both succeed.

Nobody has claimed this console. Pick a username and a password below and this becomes the administrator account — there is no setup token to prove who is allowed to claim it, so claim it now if this is the real deployment.

Claiming mints no session by itself: the screen immediately replays the same username and password through sign-in, so the account is exercised as a real login before anyone relies on it working. There is no recovery after this — no environment variable and no subcommand resets a forgotten password. The guaranteed remedy in deploy.md is stopping the container and clearing every account, which reopens this exact screen for whoever claims it next; hand-writing a new password_hash for just the locked-out account skips that reopening, but needs a tool like htpasswd that is not on every host.

This form renders for any request while the database holds no account — there is no loopback restriction here. That is the whole exposure the reversal in decisions.md accepts: both ports bind every interface, so until someone claims it, whoever reaches the port first is who claims it.

Identity mark

44 · Identity mark — inline SVG built from rects on a 24 grid, two tokens only: a --legend hairline and one --trace pip. No curve, no gradient, no fetched file, so the favicon, the rail brand and the 96px sign-in mark are one figure at three scales rather than three assets to keep in step.

The routing ladder and the logo drawn as the same figure: a bezel around a spine, three candidates entering from the left, two of them stopping at a hollow skip mark on the spine, and the third crossing it and running out to a single bright pip that breaks the frame. That is one request walking a failover chain, and it is also the instrument itself — a screen with something on it.

At 16px the interior detail drops out and what survives is a bracket, a crosshair and one dot pushed right, which is enough to find the tab by. It is the only place in the product where --trace appears without a routing decision behind it, and it is doing the same job: marking the one candidate that came out.

24 grid, rects only, no curve and no gradient. Bezel square (2,2)–(22,22) · spine 1px at x=12 · stubs 1px at y=7, y=12 and y=17, the outer two stopping at x=12 and the middle one crossing to x=19 · skip marks 3×3 hollow, centred on the spine at (12,7) and (12,17) · pip 4×4 filled, centred at (20,12), the one element in var(--accent).

One deviation from the written geometry, and it is visible above: the spine breaks either side of each skip mark instead of running unbroken from y=2 to y=22. A 3×3 outline drawn with a 1px stroke has a 1px core, which a 1px rule through it fills exactly — the two marks would render solid at every size and read as served rather than skipped, inverting the figure's meaning.
